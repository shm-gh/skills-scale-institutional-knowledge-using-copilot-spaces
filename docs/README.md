# OctoAcme Project Management Documentation

Welcome to the OctoAcme project management process documentation. This folder centralizes the project lifecycle, delivery rhythm, and communication practices used across OctoAcme initiatives. The goal is to create a single, understandable source of truth for how work is initiated, planned, executed, released, and improved over time.

OctoAcme’s approach is grounded in five core principles: customer-first thinking, iterative delivery, clear ownership, data-informed decision-making, and psychological safety. New work begins with a lightweight initiation step that validates the business need, aligns stakeholders, defines success metrics, and produces a one-pager with goals, risks, milestones, and resource expectations. Once approved, the team moves into planning, where backlog items are prioritized, estimated, and mapped into shippable increments with defined acceptance criteria and a clear definition of done.

During execution, OctoAcme uses a consistent operating rhythm to keep work moving and visible. Daily standups surface progress and blockers, weekly syncs review delivery status and dependencies, and sprint or milestone reviews provide an opportunity to inspect outcomes and adjust priorities. The project management docs define clear responsibilities for each role: Project Managers coordinate delivery, communication, and risk; Product Managers define outcomes and backlog priority; Developers build and test the work; QA validates quality and acceptance criteria; and stakeholders provide input, approvals, and strategic context. This role clarity helps avoid uncertainty while still encouraging cross-functional collaboration.

Communication is treated as a structured practice rather than an ad hoc activity. OctoAcme emphasizes single-source reporting, regular stakeholder updates, issue escalation paths, and visible risk tracking to keep everyone informed and aligned. Weekly status updates, risk registers, and escalation from team-level triage to PM/Product Lead/Sponsor all support timely decision-making when dependencies or blockers emerge. On the quality side, the process explicitly requires unit, integration, and smoke testing where appropriate, along with CI checks, security scanning, and manual QA for feature acceptance before release.

The lifecycle closes with release and retrospective stages. Release work includes staging verification, production deployment, smoke testing, and post-deploy checks, with rollback plans prepared for critical failures. After each milestone or release, the team captures what went well, what needs improvement, and which action items should be tracked in the backlog. This creates a continuous improvement loop that turns lessons learned into actionable changes and reinforces a culture of accountability, learning, and delivery discipline.

## Project Lifecycle

1. Initiation: validate the need, align stakeholders, define goals and success metrics.
2. Planning: break work into backlog items, define scope, estimate effort, and map milestones.
3. Execution: build, test, review, and iterate with regular team cadence and dependency tracking.
4. Release: validate quality, deploy, monitor outcomes, and communicate progress.
5. Close & Retrospective: capture lessons learned and convert them into concrete improvements.

## Core Roles

- Project Manager (PM): coordinates delivery, schedules, risks, and communication.
- Product Manager (PdM): defines business outcomes, prioritizes the backlog, and measures success.
- Developers: implement features, write tests, and contribute to design and review quality.
- QA/Testing: validates acceptance criteria, regression risk, and overall quality.
- Stakeholders: provide requirements, approvals, and business context.

## Quick Start for New Team Members

1. Start with the [OctoAcme Project Management Overview](octoacme-project-management-overview.md) for a high-level introduction.
2. Review the [OctoAcme Roles and Personas](octoacme-roles-and-personas.md) to understand responsibilities and expectations.
3. Use the appropriate phase-specific guide for the work you are starting:
   - Initiation for new ideas and problem validation
   - Planning for backlog, milestones, and delivery scope
   - Execution for day-to-day work and tracking
   - Release for deployment and communication
   - Retrospective for improvement and follow-up
4. Keep the project charter, risk register, and status artifacts updated so the team has a consistent source of truth.
